Five things we want you to know

Before the legal version, here's the short version. Everything below this section is more detail on the same five points.

  • Your inner work is yours to control. One setting decides whether your journal is shared with the AI or kept private on your phone — you can change it at any time in Settings. Private entries stay encrypted on your device, and we genuinely cannot read them.
  • We don't sell your data, run ads, or share with marketers. Not now, not ever. This isn't a policy we'll quietly change later — it's the reason we built Inner Map.
  • The AI providers we use don't train on your conversations. Anthropic (for chat) and OpenAI (for transcription, spoken audio, and the app's memory) process your data to power those features and then don't retain it. We don't use your data to train any model either.
  • You can export your data, or delete your account, at any time. Deletion is real — not soft-deleted, actually deleted from our servers and your device.
  • Inner Map is a reflection tool, not therapy or medical care. If you're in crisis, please reach out: 988 (US), Samaritans 116 123 (UK), or findahelpline.com (international).

The rest of this policy fills in the detail.

Information we collect

We've designed Inner Map to collect the minimum information needed to make the app work. Specifically:

Information you provide

  • Account identifier. A unique user ID generated when you first install the app. This ID isn't tied to your real name or any external account by default.
  • Sign-in identity (optional). You can use Inner Map without signing in. If you choose to sign in, we collect what's needed to recognize you: for email sign-in, your email address (we send a one-time sign-in link to it); for Apple or Google sign-in, the account reference the provider gives us and, where you share it, the email on that account. We use this to sign you in, link your account across devices, and help you recover it. We never use it for marketing.
  • Display name (required). A name or nickname, set during signup, used to address you in the app. It doesn't need to be your real name.
  • What you tell us when you set up. During onboarding the app asks what brings you to Inner Map and what you'd like to work on, and offers optional questions about your gender, relationship status, profession and age. What you answer is stored with your account and is given to the AI as context, so that it isn't starting from nothing. Every one of those questions except your name can be skipped.
  • Conversation content. What you write or say in chat with the AI. Used to generate replies and to maintain context across sessions.
  • Inner-experience map data. The parts, patterns, and inner-experience structure you map out with the AI's help. This is your work product, and we store it so you can return to it across sessions.
  • Safety-check records. The app checks what you write for signs that you may be in crisis, so it can show you support resources. When that check acts, we record that it acted, what was shown, and a short note the AI wrote about why — not the message that triggered it. The records of what we showed you are included in your data export, and all of them are deleted with your account.
  • Journal entries. A setting in Settings controls whether your entries are shared with the AI. It is on by default, and you can turn it off at any time. While it is on, entries are stored on our servers, like your conversation content, as well as on your device, so the AI can draw on them as context. While it is off, entries are stored only on your device, encrypted, and we have no copy. Changing the setting affects new entries; entries already saved keep the state they were saved with.
  • Support communications. If you contact us for support, feedback, or to lodge a complaint, we collect the information you choose to share (such as your email address and the contents of your message) to troubleshoot issues and respond to your request.

Information collected automatically

  • Usage counters. Counts of actions you take (messages sent, voice sessions started, etc.) used for rate-limiting, abuse prevention and your monthly usage allowance. Counters only — never content.
  • Device type and operating system. For compatibility and crash diagnostics.
  • App version. To know which version of the app you're using.
  • Device push token (opt-in, off by default). Only if you turn notifications on (Settings → "Notify me when something's waiting"), your device gives us a push token — an identifier that lets us deliver notifications to your phone. The notification itself never contains your content: it is a fixed message ("Something is waiting in your inbox") with no conversation, journal, or map material in it, even in hidden data fields. If you turn notifications off, we delete the token.
  • IP address. Your IP address reaches our servers with every request. We use it in passing to apply rate limits to requests made before you have signed in, and we do not store it in our database. Our hosting and content-delivery providers process it to route traffic securely and mitigate attacks, under their own retention practices. We do not use your IP address to determine your location or for advertising.

How we use AI

Inner Map relies on third-party providers to power its AI and voice features. While these providers may change over time as we improve the Service, they currently include, but are not limited to:

Anthropic (for chat)

Your text-based conversations are sent to Anthropic's API to generate AI replies. What is sent is not only the message you just typed: it includes the recent conversation, the relevant parts of your inner-experience map, any journal entries you have shared, and the past material the app has retrieved as context. Per Anthropic's API terms:

  • Customer data (your conversations) is not used to train Anthropic's models.
  • Data is processed transiently to fulfill the API request and is not retained by Anthropic beyond standard operational retention windows (typically up to 30 days for abuse monitoring, per Anthropic's data policy).
  • Anthropic does not share your data with other Anthropic customers.

OpenAI (for transcription, spoken audio, and memory)

We use OpenAI for three things: transcribing your voice notes into text, generating spoken audio (such as the "hear this spoken" feature), and creating the embeddings that power the app's memory. Embeddings are numeric representations of text that let the app find and recall relevant past context; to build them, the text of your chat messages, the AI's replies, session summaries, and any journal entries you have shared is sent to OpenAI's embeddings API. (Journal entries you keep private are never sent — see below.) Per OpenAI's API terms for paid tiers:

  • Customer data is not used to train OpenAI's models.
  • Audio and text are processed transiently to fulfill the request and are not retained by OpenAI beyond the API call.

Voice conversation providers

The live voice feature (Map Voice) turns your speech into text and speaks a reply back. To do this, the audio of what you say is sent to a speech-to-text provider (Cartesia), and the text of the reply is sent to a text-to-speech provider (ElevenLabs) to generate the audio you hear. ElevenLabs also voices the “Hear what Self would say” message in a part’s folder; if it is unavailable, that audio is generated by OpenAI instead. Chat replies you have read aloud are voiced by OpenAI. Voice notes recorded outside the live voice feature are transcribed by OpenAI, not Cartesia.

These providers process that audio or text to perform the conversion; the reply itself is written by Anthropic as described above. We do not use these providers for advertising.

Journal entries you share

While your journal is set to share, each entry's text is included in the context sent to Anthropic (for chat) so the AI can draw on it — handled exactly like your conversation content above: not used to train any model, and not retained by Anthropic beyond its standard operational window. A shared entry is also analyzed by AI to see whether it suggests anything worth adding to your inner map; any such suggestion is sent to your in-app inbox for you to accept, edit, or decline, and nothing is added to your map automatically. (Shared entries are also embedded for memory, as described under OpenAI above.) Journal entries kept private are never transmitted to Anthropic, OpenAI, or any other third party — they are not analyzed, embedded, or sent anywhere.

  • We do not have any agreements with these providers that allow them to use your data for any other purpose.

Where your data lives

On your device

  • Your private journal entries, and a copy of any entries you have shared, encrypted using AES-256. This is the only store the app encrypts itself.
  • Your app preferences and onboarding choices, in ordinary app storage, without additional encryption.
  • Your sign-in tokens and your account identifier, held in your phone's secure keystore (Apple Keychain on iOS, Android EncryptedSharedPreferences), with a working copy of the tokens in ordinary app storage.
  • The identifiers of up to ten accounts this device has previously used, in ordinary app storage, so that an account you were signed out of can still be found. These are cleared when you delete your account.

Your conversations are not kept on your device. They are stored on our servers and loaded when you open the app, so there is no offline copy of your chat history on the phone.

The encryption key for your journal is generated on your device at first launch and stored in your phone's secure keystore. The key never leaves your device. We do not have access to it. We cannot decrypt your private journal entries even if compelled to do so. Because this data and the encryption key reside locally on your device, you are solely responsible for preventing unauthorized access to your device (e.g., by using a strong passcode or biometric lock).

On our servers

  • Your account record and settings
  • Your conversation history (used for AI context)
  • A working copy of your conversations and shared journal entries, held alongside the numeric representations the app uses to find relevant past material
  • Your shared journal entries — only while your journal is set to share (entries written while it is off never reach our servers)
  • Your inner-experience map data (parts, wounds, patterns), the history of the belief you've been building, and any readings written for you
  • Your safety-check records
  • A record of any sign-in that landed on a different account than the one this device was using, so that a stranded account can still be identified
  • Usage counters

All server-side data is stored on Railway's managed PostgreSQL. Railway encrypts it at rest, and all communication between your device and our servers uses TLS (HTTPS/WSS) encryption in transit.

Transient processing (third parties, not retained)

  • AI replies are generated via Anthropic and OpenAI APIs as described in "How we use AI" above. Your data passes through these providers transiently for the purpose of generating responses and is not retained by them beyond their standard operational windows.

How we protect your data

We've designed Inner Map with security-by-design and privacy-by-design principles. Specific measures include:

  • Encryption in transit: All API calls and WebSocket connections use TLS 1.2+ encryption.
  • Encryption at rest: Our hosting provider encrypts the server database at rest. Private and shared journal entries are encrypted on your device (AES-256) by the app itself.
  • Access control on your data: Any request that reads or writes the content in your account — your conversations, journal, map and settings — is tied to your account identifier, and requests without it are rejected. (Feedback you send us is the one exception: you can send it without being signed in.)
  • Rate limiting: Multiple layers of rate limits prevent abuse of API endpoints (per-user daily caps on chat messages, voice sessions, transcriptions, and data exports).
  • Anti-brute-force: Invite code endpoints have failure-rate limits.
  • Log hygiene: We do not log the content of your conversations, journal entries, or your name. Our server logs record that something happened and how large it was, not what it said, and an automated check in our build process fails if a new log line would carry your content.
  • Access control: Only essential Inner Map personnel have access to production systems.
  • Backup encryption: Database backups are encrypted and retained according to our hosting provider's policies.
  • Breach notification: In the event of a security incident affecting your data, we will notify affected users promptly and as required by applicable law.

User Responsibility: You are responsible for protecting your account credentials and limiting access to your devices. Inner Map will never send you an email requesting your account identifiers. A sign-in link we email you is a credential — treat it like a password and do not forward it. If you receive a suspicious email claiming to be from us, please notify support@my-inner-map.com.

We periodically review our security practices internally. As Inner Map grows, we plan to engage external auditors for formal annual reviews.

No system is perfectly secure. We can't promise we'll never have an incident, but we can promise we'll handle one transparently and quickly.

How long we keep your data

We keep your data only as long as we have a reason to.

  • Account data and inner-experience map: Kept while your account is active. Deleted within 30 days of account deletion.
  • Conversation history: Kept while your account is active. Used for AI session memory.
  • Private journal entries: Stored on your device. We have no copies and cannot retrieve them after they're deleted from your phone.
  • Shared journal entries: Kept on our servers while your account is active (like your conversation history), deleted immediately when you delete the entry, and within 30 days of account deletion.
  • Usage counters: Kept for as long as your account exists. They are what the app uses for rate limits, abuse prevention and your monthly allowance, and they are deleted with your account.
  • Database backups: Retained according to our hosting provider's policies.

When you delete your account, your data is removed from our active systems immediately and from backups within 30 days.

De-identified Data: We reserve the right to retain and use de-identified and aggregated data indefinitely after your account is deleted. This data will no longer contain any personally identifiable information and is used strictly for analytics, research, and improving the Service.

Your rights and choices

You have the following rights regarding your data:

Access

You can request a copy of the data we hold about you on our servers. The app has a built-in export function: Settings → Privacy, Data & Safety → Export My Data. The export is delivered as a JSON file via your phone's share sheet (so you can save it to Files, send it to yourself via email, etc.). The file is written to your phone so it can be shared, and the app removes it once you have.

The export contains your content and activity: your account and settings, your conversations, your inner-experience map, the history of the belief you've been building, any readings the app has written for you, journal entries shared with the AI, your activity timeline, session summaries, a record of the times the app's safety check paused the conversation, offered you a referral, offered a quieter line, or checked back in with you, and usage counters. Some records are not in the file: sign-in identity records, notification tokens, in-app inbox cards, feedback you've submitted, subscription and credit records, internal diagnostic logs, records of sign-in collisions, and the working copy of your conversations and shared journal entries that the app keeps alongside the numeric representations it uses to find relevant past material. If you'd like a copy of those too, email privacy@my-inner-map.com and we'll provide them. Everything — in the export file or not — is permanently removed when you delete your account.

Deletion

You can delete your entire account from within the app. Tap Settings, then Delete account under ACCOUNT — or open Privacy, Data & Safety and use Delete My Account there. Either route opens the same confirmation screen.

This removes your data from our active systems immediately. It cascades to all related records — sessions, parts, journal entries (including any you shared with the AI) and the material the app kept to recall them, sign-in identity, notification tokens, your in-app inbox, and feedback — and triggers your device to clear its local storage, including the encrypted journal, your sign-in tokens and your account identifier.

Deletion is permanent. We cannot restore an account once deleted.

Correction

If something we've stored is wrong (an email address typo, for example), you can correct it via Settings, or by contacting privacy@my-inner-map.com.

Withdrawal of consent

If you've consented to specific uses of your data (for example, notifications you've turned on), you can withdraw that consent at any time by turning the feature off or removing the relevant data in Settings. Turning notifications off deletes your push token from our servers.

Portability

The export function described above gives you a machine-readable copy of your data so you can move it elsewhere if you choose.

Objection & Restriction

If you object to any specific processing we do, or wish to restrict processing, please contact privacy@my-inner-map.com.

When we might say no to a request

We will respect your privacy rights to the fullest extent possible. There are a few cases where we may not be able to fully honor a request:

  • When we need the data to keep the service running. For example, we cannot delete your active account while you're still using it; you'd need to delete the account first.

  • When fulfilling the request would affect another person's privacy or safety. For example, where your data is bound up with someone else's in a shared record, we may need to handle the deletion in a way that protects them too.

If we cannot fulfill a request, we will tell you why and offer an alternative where possible.
